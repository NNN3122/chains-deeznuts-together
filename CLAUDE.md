# CHAINS DEEZNUTS TOGETHER — project guide

Read this before touching anything. It captures the full vision so every session builds toward the same game, and the rules that keep the codebase coherent.

## What this game is

A 1–6 player online co-op horror-comedy. Players are physically chained together and must cooperate to climb upward through stacked horror levels and escape at the top. The tone is chaotic and stupid-funny, but the horror is real: tense atmosphere, monsters that kill you, and lots of jump scares. The rhythm is: scream, then laugh.

- **Camera:** toggle first-person / third-person at any time (key V).
- **Chains:** physics-based. One player falling, jumping, or panicking pulls the others. Falling drags teammates down. Dying sends the whole group back to the last checkpoint. Chain topology changes with player count (line, ring, pairs).
- **Levels, stacked vertically (bottom to top):**
  1. Liminal yellow office maze — buzzing fluorescents, shifting layouts, an entity you hear but rarely see.
  2. Abandoned pizzeria — possessed animatronic mascots; manage doors, cameras and power while climbing.
  3. Infected rooftop city — rooftop parkour; slow infected by day, fast lethal hunters at night.
  4. Seductive hotel — dim luxury; succubi lurk (see Enemies).
  5. Final mashup ascent — every zone remixed, ending in a boss chase to the exit.
- **Items ("go stupid with it"):** random loot. Guns (pistol, shotgun, ridiculous oversized weapons), bombs and sticky explosives (hurt monsters and teammates alike), energy drink (speed boost, jittery screen, then a crash), jetpack (lifts the whole chain, hard to control), grappling hook, flashlight, flare gun, decoy noisemaker, and a rare **Transformation item** that turns a player into an original superhero archetype (bald one-punch hero, speedster, flying strongman) or an animal (snail: slow, sticks to walls; bird: flies but weak; cat: silent, lands on feet, nine lives). Transformations affect the whole chained team in funny ways (a bird trying to carry five people).
- **Enemies:** every level has its own monster plus roaming threats. Items can fight, stun, distract, or help escape them. Monsters react to noise. Boss encounters end select levels.
- **Succubi:** from a distance they look like friendly women calling for help; when a player gets close they transform into demons and attack. Observant players can spot subtle tells (glitchy audio, wrong reflections, flickering or wrong shadows). Strictly non-explicit: the horror is the betrayal and the transformation.
- **Horror:** scripted jump scares plus randomized/procedural scares so repeat runs stay unpredictable. Proximity voice chat later; monsters hear player noise (including microphones, eventually).
- **Originality:** all characters, monsters, heroes, mascots and names are original designs. Never copy or name existing franchises or characters in code, assets, or text.

The full game design document is `design/GAME_DESIGN_DOCUMENT.md`. Treat it as the reference for names and numbers; this file is the short version.

## Engine and architecture rules

- **Godot 4.3+ with GDScript** (developed and tested on 4.7.2). Static typing everywhere. Tabs for indentation.
- **Multiplayer:** Godot high-level multiplayer. Solo uses `OfflineMultiplayerPeer` (same server code path, no sockets); co-op uses `ENetMultiplayerPeer`. **Host-authoritative:** the host (peer 1) simulates all bodies, the chain, items, monsters and triggers; clients send input by RPC and render replicated state. Built for 6 players; never special-case "2 players".
- **Assets:** CC0 textures live in `assets/textures/` with their sources in `assets/textures/LICENSE.md` (ceiling tiles and carpet from TextureCan, mapped in world space by `LevelKit` shaders, carpet recoloured blue to mustard). Everything else is built in code: shader materials (`LevelKit`), primitive models (`PlayerModel`, `ItemModels`), synthesized sounds (`Sfx`), system fonts (`UiTheme`). Only download new assets with the user's explicit OK, CC0 or similar only, and record each one in the LICENSE file. Texture imports: VRAM compressed, mipmaps on, normal maps flagged as normal maps.
- **Folders** (each system grows without touching the others):
  - `src/core/` main flow, `GameState` autoload (run state + input map), `Settings` (static, saved to user://settings.cfg)
  - `src/net/` `Net` autoload: solo / host / join, spawners for players, pickups and thrown objects, the Ballast switch
  - `src/player/` `Player` (controller, noise, grab state), `PlayerInput` (remote-desktop-proof input), `PlayerModel`
  - `src/chain/` `ChainBody` base, `Ballast` (solo partner), `ChainSystem` (topology, ledge wrapping, pull, hang drag, link rendering, jerks)
  - `src/items/` data-driven items: one `ItemData` .tres per item in `data/` plus a logic script extending `ItemBase`; `ItemRegistry` finds them; `ItemPickup`, `Thrown`, `ItemModels`
  - `src/enemies/` `HollowHire` (Level 1: one on B4 and two or three on each floor above, wander the maze cell to cell via `YellowRooms.route`, hunt by SIGHT (view cone + line of sight; crouching shortens the range, a lit flashlight lengthens it), chase at 5.6 m/s, pin a caught player; a flashlight in the face stuns for 3 s, two Snubby rounds or a bomb kill, `YellowRooms._tick_hires` respawns a dead one out of sight after 45 s; Break Rooms are safe; the Custodian is gone), `PappyPepperoni` (the pizzeria's chef animatronic: moves one node per Step only while nobody looks at him and no live camera shows his node; lunges and takes the nearest player; a held shutter makes him bang three times and go home), `Launcher` (the Jumper: Level 3's sofa-sized spider; scuttles at 1.3 m/s, screeches and coils 0.6 s, jumps 5-6 m along the line it had when it coiled; a hit pins the victim; bracing teammates, a flashlight, the air horn or two Snubby hits free them; three hits or a bomb kill it; at a roof's edge it looks across for the next roof and swings over on a thread (`SWING`, `swing_from`/`swing_to` replicated so every peer draws the thread), climbs a wall face when its target is above (`CLIMB`), and falls to its death if a jump ends over nothing). `src/enemies/horde/`: the crowd tech, see below.
  - `src/levels/` `LevelStack` (the whole building, group "level": `YellowRooms` with `Pizzeria` built directly on top; forwards floor names, ceilings, scare pools, layoffs and regroups to whichever level a position is in), `YellowRooms` (Level 1 generator, the forced route events and the power stutter), `StuckDoor`, `AlarmPull`, `TimedHatch` (the route pieces that force a sprint, a pull and a run), `Pizzeria` + `PizzeriaProps` (Level 2 generator: fixed room plans per floor, Pappy's node graph, power bank, night clock, generator, jukebox, its own scares), `FreightCar` (the B1 elevator that rides up into Receiving), `Shutter`, `SecurityDesk`, `Dumbwaiter`, `StageLift`, `LevelKit` (materials, environment, `BoxBatch`), `LightController`, plate, lift, checkpoint, `HordeArena` (Level 3, Carrow Heights: the Loading Lot (a flat block with a knee-high parapet, a fence, a gap, four Jumpers and the first two waves) and then the district: `ROOF_COUNT` 11 rooftops laid out north of the lot from the seed by `_lay_out_route` (`Roof`: rect, height, `Link` JUMP 2.4-3.4 m gap / PLANK or PIPE 4-6.5 m, level, flush with both roofs / DROP a storey down / CLIMB a unit against a 2.6-3.5 m higher wall; heights stay 0-14 m over the lot so the Stack can tell the roof from the pizzeria), two vents per roof kept out of the crossing lanes, a Jumper on roofs 3, 6 and 9, checkpoints on roofs 4 and 8, and the tower butted against the last roof: a staircase of solid landings (`escape_steps`, one mantle each, walked into from the side) up to the helipad and the helicopter. The street is 38 m down and has no floor: any body that has been up here (`_been_up`) and drops below it is a layoff. Built under a root at `origin`, with the city round it from `_build_city` (a ring of tenements round the lot, rows flanking the route, a far skyline, billboards; all boxes in the batch, nothing the crowd walks on), lit by the moon and sodium lamps under `LevelKit.make_night_sky` (a sky shader: gradient, twinkling stars, a moon, two layers of drifting cloud; its noise hash has no sin() because sin rounding at large inputs made every cell edge a seam); embedded in `LevelStack` as the roof at y = pizzeria top + 1 with its pad over the prop loft and floor index 8, checkpoint 7; or standalone with `--arena`, when it is the "level" node with the same surface as `LevelStack`)
  - `src/scare/` `ScareDirector` autoload (scripted + procedural scares), `ScareTrigger`
  - `src/fx/` `Effects` (RPC visuals and sounds the server triggers on every peer)
  - `src/audio/` `Sfx` (procedural sound bank; silent when headless)
  - `src/ui/` title, settings, pause, end screen, HUD, `NightShiftPanel` (pizzeria clock, power, Pappy status, the camera feed through a SubViewport), `ScreenFx` post shader, menu backdrop, `UiTheme`
  - `tests/` smoke test and screenshot tour, loaded by path only with `--smoke` / `--shots`
  - `tools/` (ignored by Godot) `make_friend_zip.ps1`, `publish_release.ps1`, `write_licenses.gd`, and `friend_package/` (the READ ME shipped in the zip, HOW TO SEND for the user)
- **Items are data-driven:** adding an item = one `.tres` in `src/items/data/` + one logic script + a model case in `ItemModels`. Nothing else changes. Players carry three (`Player.slots`); only the selected item gets the use input (`ItemBase.server_tick(delta, held, tapped, reload_tapped)`), the others tick idle; `on_deselect` switches anything "on" off. An item that should pour into another (ammo) implements `merge_into(player)`. Dropped pickups keep `carry_state()`.
- **Levels are generated from a seed** so every peer builds the identical world before connecting. Replicated props need identical node names on all peers. The seed is the host's: `Net.world_config` ({seed, arena, horde}) is random from the title (`Net.random_world_config`) and `YellowRooms.DEFAULT_SEED` 1931 from the command line (`--seed=N` overrides; the tests rely on the fixed one). A joiner builds the default world, connects, and sends its config in `register_player`; a host with a different config answers `_rpc_wrong_world` and drops it, and `Main._on_world_mismatch` tears the world down, rebuilds with the host's config and joins again (once). This keeps the rule that the world exists before any replication traffic. The office layout, the pizzeria's dressing and the Lot's crowd all take the seed. A joiner's console shows a few harmless "Node not found ... /Sync" errors in the second before it rebuilds (the host's synchronizers name nodes the default world doesn't have). The whole Stack is built at once (`LevelStack`): the pizzeria sits at y = 20 (4 office floors x 5 m), positioned so its Receiving room is directly over B1's freight shaft; both levels use 5 m floors so `LightController` floor indices stay global (office 0-3, pizzeria 4-6). Only the floors next to the camera are drawn.
- **Level 1 rules:** the Hollow Hires replaced the Custodian (the user found sneaking through a sound-only hunter boring). They see; sound only draws them to look. Nothing in the office hunts by noise any more, so the noise meter is a "they'll come and look" meter there. Monsters that stun answer `stun(seconds)`; the flashlight burn checks live in each monster. Level 1 must not be crouch-walkable (the user's second playtest): the Hires patrol the route (`YellowRooms.patrol_cells`; Hire 0 of each floor is the `sentry` for the last three route rooms, the rest pick route rooms 65% of the time), end walks with a 2.4 s `SWEEP` through 180 degrees, and a fresh sighting calls the floor (`YellowRooms.hire_callout` -> `HollowHire.join_chase`); loud events call `wake_floor(i, pos, seconds)` -> `alert`, which makes them hunt the nearest player's room with sharper eyes for that long. Every floor forces noise and running (`_build_events`): `StuckDoor` on a bridge doorway of B4's and B1's route (opens only to a body over 5 m/s, bang = noise 100 for 3 s, wake 14 s), `AlarmPull` on B3 (the lift's `PressurePlate.enabled` is false until it's pulled: hold Ctrl 0.6 s; klaxon and floor strobe 40 s, wake 40 s), `TimedHatch` over B2's shaft (release switch two rooms back, open 25 s, slams unless a body is in or at the hole), and the B1 ambush (`_tick_ambush`: first player into the elevator room, a Hire is placed in the last doorway and screams; the car's inside is `is_safe_spot`). A squeaky decoy is heard floor-wide and a Hire stands over it sweeping until it stops. Darkness: office ambient 0.045, troffers 0.85, 44% of ordinary tubes flicker/dying/dead, auto-exposure max 105; `lights_out` is 1.4 s gutter + 12-20 s dark; `_tick_stutter` blacks out the Line's floor 12-20 s every 60-90 s (not mid-chase); `LightController.floor_stutter`, and tubes registered `safe` (Break Rooms) never black out. Blasts kill monsters on their own floor only.
- **Level 2 rules:** `Pizzeria.nearest_node` prefers a node in the position's own room over a closer one through a wall, and Pappy's Step goes round by the graph when the nearest player is behind a wall (he used to stand at the wall forever). Pappy is host-authoritative like the Custodian: `watched` (any player's view cone + line of sight, or a live desk whose camera covers his node) freezes his Step timer; Steps teleport him one graph node toward the nearest player, never seen moving. Shutters close only while someone braces at the pull chain (`Shutter.closed`, replicated); desks clip in whoever stands on the painted spot and F cycles cameras instead of using the item; the power bank (`Pizzeria.power`, replicated) drains with live desks and the rising lift, and standing 3 s in a Break Room restarts it. At 0% every pizzeria light is out and Pappy steps every 3 s regardless of eyes. Monsters that hunt a player expose `hunt_peer` and sit in group "monster" (Hires also keep group "hire", Launchers "launcher", Pappy "pappy").
- **The horde (`src/enemies/horde/`, group "horde", not "monster"):** a crowd is arrays, not nodes. `Horde` keeps up to 2000 agents in packed arrays (x, z, y, velocity, yaw, walk phase, state, kind) and steps them in one loop on `HordeField`, a flow field on a 1 m grid rebuilt in slices (a few hundred cells per tick, breadth-first flood then per-cell gradient directions, swapped in whole) toward every chained body and anything loud (a decoy). Separation runs through a spatial hash; crowding lifts an agent (`climb`) so a wall of bodies climbs itself. Every agent moves and animates every tick (slicing the mass to a third per tick made the crowd step at 20 Hz, which the user saw as lag); LOD: the nearest 250 to the Line are "active" and recompute separation every tick with claws, lunges, hands on the chain and shootability; the mass recomputes separation a third per tick and reuses the last push. The walk cycle follows ground covered, with a floor of 70% of the meant pace so a pile pressed against a wall keeps working its legs; heading turns at 5 rad/s, never snaps; the shader adds TIME-driven sway, head twitches, a limp for some, and arms up (custom.z + 2) for active agents within 3 m. Rendering is one MultiMesh per kind (`HordeMesh`: Roach, Weaver, Flitter, all original mutations; `Horde.FLYING` kinds skip walls and the climb, circle at `FLY_HEIGHT` over the nearest player and dive (`DIVE_*`), never grab the chain, and are hit at `HIT_HEIGHTS` per kind) whose vertex shader animates limbs from the mesh's limb tags (UV2, CUSTOM0/1 pivots) and four floats of INSTANCE_CUSTOM (phase, seed, tint, state); the whole buffer is written once per tick. The seed also gives every body its own build (length, width, leg length, head), a limp for some, and its own chitin hue, so no two in the swarm match. Hits are capsule overlaps against players, never physics: at the edge of a crowd the claws shove you out, three sets of hands on one player for 2.5 s is a layoff. Hands on the chain: an active agent within 1 m of a segment hangs on, each holder takes 30% of both ends' speed (`Player.hold_drag`, set and consumed per tick), three stop the team; a body over 4.6 m/s (a sprint with one holder, a slide), the chain snapping taut (`ChainSystem.jerked`) or the air horn fling them off; the Snubby asks `Horde.hit_ray` before the physics ray so a round drops the nearest active agent; bombs call `blast`. Rooftops: `HordeField` cells are FLOOR (with a ground `height`), WALL or VOID (the street; `fill_void` then `set_floor_rect` per roof), walkers stand on their cell's height and can't take a step up over 0.6 m (a pile can climb over a low one), and `CLIMBER` kinds (Weavers) climb a higher roof's face (`CLIMB`, `CLIMB_SPEED`) or, at a VOID edge, look across with `floor_ahead` and swing over on a thread (`SWING`, drawn as lines from `swing_anchor`; clients replay swings from snapshots). With no field under them (the Line is on another roof) climbers head straight for the nearest player and roaches mill. A walker more than `LEFT_BEHIND` 110 m from every chained body goes back into the walls (GONE, server-side, carried to clients by snapshots), so the swarm left on crossed roofs costs nothing. `MAX_AGENTS` 2400. Net: the server sends the active set at 10 Hz (8 bytes per agent, the far half every other send) plus 48 of the mass round-robin, unreliable; clients run the same mass from the same spawn seed and their own field and correct toward snapshots. Every level that hosts a horde builds the field (`block_box` for walls) and spawns with a fixed seed on every peer.
- **The slide:** sprint (over 4.5 m/s) then Ctrl in `Player._tick_normal`: capsule to crouch height, 8.4 m/s decaying on a square curve over 1 s, no steering beyond a nudge, ends standing if there is headroom and Ctrl is up, else crouched. `SFLAG_SLIDING` drives the pose, the camera dip and tilt, and the scrape sound on every peer. A slide under a Launcher's jump counts as a dodge, and throws horde holders off the chain.
- **Physics layers:** 1 world, 2 chain bodies (players, Ballast), 4 thrown, 8 monster hitboxes. Areas that detect players use mask 2.
- **Server-only logic** is guarded by `multiplayer.is_server()`; anything clients see is pushed with `call_local` RPCs or replicated properties.
- **Autoloads:** `GameState`, `Net`, `ScareDirector`, `Voice` only. `Voice` needs `audio/driver/enable_input` (project.godot) and does nothing headless or solo.
- **The user plays over TeamViewer.** TeamViewer sends a held key as a stream of taps and breaks captured mouse look. Movement must never depend on mouse capture; keys are bound by physical code AND key label; Remote Controls mode (auto-on when TeamViewer is running or key flicker is detected) holds keys for 650 ms after each tap. Its mouse look hides the cursor and confines it to the window (`MOUSE_MODE_CONFINED_HIDDEN`), with no button to hold: plain movement turns the view, and holding the cursor in the outer 7% of the window keeps turning, since a remote cursor stops at the edge of the user's own screen. The user rejected right-drag look ("like all games"); don't bring it back. Arrow keys walk/turn and R toggles auto-walk in every mode.
- Keep the chain central: a mechanic, item or level piece that would play the same without the chain is wrong for this game.

## Running and testing

- Double-click `PLAY.bat` or the desktop shortcut. `PLAY 2 WINDOWS (co-op test).bat` opens a host and a client side by side. `RUN TESTS.bat` runs the smoke tests.
- **Sharing with friends:** `MAKE FRIEND ZIP.bat` runs `tools/make_friend_zip.ps1`. It writes `CHAINS DEEZNUTS TOGETHER - send to friends/` on the Desktop:

- GDScript gotcha: generic `floor()`, `abs()`, `min()` return Variant, so `var x := floor(y)` fails to compile. Use `floorf`, `absf`, `minf`.

## Conventions

- One class per file, `class_name` for anything referenced by type. File names in `snake_case`, class names in `PascalCase`.
- Signals over polling for UI; polling is fine inside physics ticks.
- Constants for tunables at the top of each script with a short comment on the unit (meters, seconds, m/s).
- Comments explain intent and gotchas, not what the line does.
- Keep `CLAUDE.md` current when the architecture or status changes.
